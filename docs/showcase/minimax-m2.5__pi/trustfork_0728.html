## Comprehensive Summary

The **2026 Enterprise Digital Transformation White Paper** examines how cloud computing, artificial intelligence, big data, and digital workplace technologies are reshaping enterprise operations and competitiveness. Based on a survey of **500 enterprises**, it covers adoption trends, developments in major industry sectors, implementation barriers, recommended actions, and the outlook for emerging technologies.

### Main Topics Covered

- Adoption of cloud computing, AI, and data-driven management
- Normalization of hybrid and remote work
- Digital transformation in manufacturing, marketing, and enterprise services
- Data security, talent shortages, and legacy-system modernization
- Future opportunities involving AI agents and digital twins

### Key Findings

- **Cloud adoption reached 78%**, representing a **15% increase from the previous year**. Cloud computing is now a central part of enterprise technology strategy.
- **AI penetration reached 45%**, with applications concentrated in customer service, marketing, risk management, and related business scenarios.
- **62% of enterprises now make data-driven decisions**, indicating that analytics and big-data capabilities are becoming more integrated into management.
- **85% of enterprises have adopted hybrid work**, showing that remote work has shifted from a temporary arrangement to a standard operating model.

### Sector Analysis

**Smart Manufacturing**

Industry 4.0 adoption continues to accelerate. Investment in smart-factory construction increased by **30%**, while supply-chain digitization emerged as a major investment priority. The report forecasts that the smart manufacturing market will exceed **500 billion yuan in 2026**.

**Digital Marketing**

Live-streaming e-commerce remains a major growth channel, recording an annual growth rate of **120%**. Private-traffic operations, in which brands cultivate and communicate directly with owned customer communities, have become a standard marketing practice. Short-video campaigns now account for **more than 40% of total digital marketing budgets**.

**Enterprise Services**

The SaaS market continues to expand at a **compound annual growth rate above 25%**. Low-code and no-code platforms are helping enterprises accelerate application development, while the number of users of enterprise collaboration tools has surpassed **500 million**.

### Principal Challenges

The report identifies three major obstacles:

1. **Data security and privacy:** Risks are becoming more prominent as organizations collect, share, and depend on greater volumes of data.
2. **Digital talent shortages:** Enterprises lack professionals who combine technical expertise with business and industry knowledge.
3. **Legacy-system transformation:** Modernizing traditional systems remains technically difficult, expensive, and operationally risky.

### Recommendations

The white paper advises enterprises to:

- Establish comprehensive data-security governance covering organizational policies, technical safeguards, and privacy protection.
- Increase investment in recruiting and developing multidisciplinary digital talent.
- Modernize systems progressively instead of attempting abrupt, large-scale replacement, thereby controlling costs and reducing transition risks.

### Conclusions and Outlook

The paper concludes that enterprise digital transformation is entering a **“deep-water” phase** in 2026. The focus is shifting from basic technology adoption toward effective implementation, integration with business processes, and measurable value creation. Cloud, AI, data analytics, and hybrid-work systems have already achieved substantial adoption, but further progress will depend on resolving security, workforce, and legacy-technology constraints.

Looking ahead, **AI agents and digital twins** are expected to become important new growth areas. Overall, enterprises that combine technology investment with security governance, talent development, and phased modernization will be best positioned to convert digital transformation into sustained competitive advantage.
